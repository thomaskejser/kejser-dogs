Many dogs eat grass, and in most cases it is normal behaviour.
The behaviour is also observed in wolves, the ancestors of dogs.

## What the research shows

In a survey of [1,571 dog owners](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2007.05.018):

- 68% of dogs ate plants daily or weekly, and grass was the most common plant;
- only 9% often seemed ill before eating plants;
- only 22% often vomited afterwards.

Younger dogs ate plants more often and were even less likely to seem unwell.
Diet made no difference: dogs whose food included vegetables or fruit still ate plants.
The researchers concluded that plant eating is a normal behaviour of dogs, not usually a sign of illness.

## Do dogs eat grass to make themselves sick?

Probably not.
In an [experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2009.12.003), dogs ate **less** grass while they had a mild upset stomach with loose stools, and they vomited only twice in 374 bouts of grass eating.
Other kinds of upset, such as nausea, were not tested.

## Possible explanations

- **Inherited behaviour.** Wild canids regularly eat plant material, especially grass: it has been found in up to 74% of wolf droppings in some studies.
- **Getting rid of worms.** The survey's authors suggest that eating grass may once have helped wild canids expel intestinal parasites; this is a hypothesis, not a finding.

Owners often suggest taste or boredom, but these have not been studied.

## When to call the vet

- Your dog suddenly eats grass frantically
- It vomits often, or seems unwell
- The grass may have been treated with pesticides or fertiliser

In one reported case, a dog that had eaten plants and vomited every day for seven years [stopped within three days](https://doi.org/10.1292/jvms.69.779) of moving to a high-fibre diet, so persistent grass eating with vomiting is worth discussing with your vet.
