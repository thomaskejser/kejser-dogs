Like people, ageing dogs can develop **canine cognitive dysfunction**, a dementia-like condition that causes confusion, disturbed sleep and changes in behaviour.

It is common and often missed:

- In one study, an estimated [14% of older dogs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tvjl.2009.11.007) had it, but only 1.9% had been diagnosed by a vet.
- It tends to get worse: over two years, a [third of older dogs](https://doi.org/10.1111/jvim.13633) with normal thinking developed mild impairment, and 22% of those with mild impairment developed the full condition.

## What the research shows

Data from more than [15,000 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-15837-9) in the Dog Aging Project showed that:

- the odds of cognitive dysfunction rose by 52% with each year of age;
- dogs reported as not active had 6.47 times the odds of very active dogs of the same age, health and breed type.

A [second analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-022-00655-8) of 11,574 dogs from the project found the same link between activity and cognitive health.
A survey of [858 older dogs](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2026.1833531) found that dogs with a lifelong sports career, or that did activities with their owner, had less severe decline.

## Experiments in laboratory dogs

A study followed 24 older beagles for more than two years on an enriched life, a diet rich in antioxidants, or both.
The combination [reduced the build-up of amyloid](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.6194-09.2010) in the brain, a protein linked to dementia, but enrichment on its own did not.
Exercise was only a small part of that enrichment: two 20-minute walks a week, alongside living in pairs, new toys and daily brain training.
I could not find an independent study of how this enrichment affected the dogs' learning; the one I found was co-written by a pet-food company employee, which I don't cite.
No study has yet tested exercise on its own.

## A caution

The Dog Aging Project studies looked at dogs at a single point in time, so they cannot tell cause from effect.
Dogs in cognitive decline may simply become less active.
As the authors put it, future studies need to follow the same dogs over time and test exercise directly.
The Dog Aging Project follows tens of thousands of dogs over many years, which should help answer that question.

## What you can do

Keep an older dog active at a level that suits its health, and mention any confusion or behaviour changes to your vet, since the condition is often missed.
