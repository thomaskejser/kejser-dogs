Rat poison is toxic to dogs.
Most rat and mouse poisons are **anticoagulants**: they stop the blood from clotting, causing internal bleeding.
In Norway, anticoagulants were involved in [79% of animal poisonings](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0278642) by rat poison, and 93% of those animals were dogs.

Untreated bleeding can be fatal, but most treated dogs recover.

## Signs of rat poison poisoning

Bleeding usually starts [three to five days](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2022.879179) after eating the poison, so a dog can seem well at first.
It can happen almost anywhere in the body.
The most common [signs at the vet](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2226-4348) were:

- Lethargy (79%)
- Pale gums (79%)
- Not eating (60%)
- Difficulty or fast breathing (60%)
- Bruising, a swollen belly, or blood in faeces or urine, which were less common

## How dangerous is it?

- **Treatment works**: [98.7% of poisoned dogs survived](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.242.4.516) in one study.
- **Dogs that are already bleeding** do less well: [87% survived](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2022.879179).
- **A blood transfusion matters**: among bleeding dogs, about 4 in 10 that did not get one died, against 1 in 25 that did.
- **Newer poisons work differently**: bromethalin, a rat poison that attacks the nervous system, is [increasingly used](https://doi.org/10.1111/jvim.70057) and causes seizures, tremors, weakness and paralysis.
  Only [25 of 192 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1111/vec.13026) that ate it became ill, but those with severe signs were often put to sleep.

### At what dose do symptoms appear?

This depends on the product.
In one study, the amount of poison found in the blood did not predict how ill a dog was.

## What to do?

Call your vet or an animal poison hotline straight away.
Note which product was eaten, roughly how much, and when.
Call even if your dog seems well.
Bring the packaging, since the treatment depends on the type of poison.

### What will the Vet do?

Vets will check how well your dog's blood clots, and give the antidote, **vitamin K1**.
Given into a vein, it brought clotting back to normal [within 24 hours](https://doi.org/10.1111/vec.70120) in every dog in one study, and 94% of dogs in another went home after a median of three days.
A dog that is bleeding may also be given plasma or a blood transfusion.
