Blue-green algae is toxic to dogs.
These algae (cyanobacteria) grow in lakes, ponds and slow rivers, and some produce potent toxins.

Poisoning often kills dogs, sometimes very quickly.

## Signs of algae poisoning

There are two main kinds of toxin, and they act at different speeds:

- **Nerve toxins** act within minutes: dogs have [died within an hour](https://doi.org/10.1177/104063870802000119) of swimming, after seizures.
- **Liver toxins** act over days: the illness [lasted 1 to 7 days](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins5091597) in most cases.

Signs reported in poisoned dogs include:

- Vomiting and diarrhoea
- Lethargy and weakness
- Stumbling, restlessness or a change in behaviour
- Deep breathing, paralysis or convulsions

## How dangerous is it?

- **Poisoning is often fatal**: 58% of the cases in a US surveillance system died, and 83% of dogs in older reports.
- **Dogs are especially at risk** because they swim in and drink from water during algal blooms, eat mats of algae, and lick algae off their fur afterwards.
- **Cases are rising**: the share of calls to a large US animal poison centre that concerned algae [rose more than tenfold](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins15080505) between 2010 and 2022.

### At what dose do symptoms appear?

The amount of toxin varies from bloom to bloom, so there is no safe amount.
Keep dogs away from water with scum on the surface or a bad smell.
But clear water is not proof of safety: in Berlin, at least 12 dogs were poisoned at a lake where [no bloom was visible](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins10020060), because the algae grew among floating water plants.

## What to do?

Rinse your dog with clean water if it has been in water that might be affected.
Go to a vet immediately if your dog becomes ill after swimming.
Note where your dog swam, and when.

### What will the Vet do?

There is no antidote, but supportive care can save dogs: [five of six dogs](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins11080456) poisoned by liver toxins survived with it.
Prompt treatment matters, so don't wait to see if your dog gets better.
