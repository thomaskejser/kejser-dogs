Onions and garlic are toxic to dogs, as are leeks, chives and their relatives.
They contain **sulphur compounds** that damage red blood cells, causing anaemia.

Severe anaemia can be fatal, though deaths are rarely reported.

## Signs of onion and garlic poisoning

Signs can appear one or several days after eating.
In dogs fed onion, red blood cells [began to fall after one day](https://doi.org/10.1177/030098588502200414) and reached their lowest point around day 5; with garlic, the lowest point came at days 9 to 11.

- Vomiting, diarrhoea and loss of appetite
- Lethargy and weakness
- Pale gums and fast breathing
- Red or brown urine

## How dangerous is it?

The risk depends on how much is eaten, in what form, and on the dog:

- **Raw, cooked, dried and powdered** onion and garlic are all toxic, as are foods made with them: cooking and drying [do not remove the toxin](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2016.00026).
- **Some dogs are more sensitive**: dogs with an inherited red blood cell type were [far more susceptible](https://doi.org/10.2460/ajvr.1992.53.01.134) to onion.
  This type is found in [26 to 38%](https://doi.org/10.1292/jvms.59.495) of Shiba Inus and Akitas, and 42% of Korean Jindos.
- **Fatal cases are rare but do happen**: a dog died after eating [raw garlic](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16111712), with 16 g of garlic cloves found in its stomach.

### At what dose do symptoms appear?

Changes in the blood appear after a dog eats [15 to 30 g of onion per kg](https://doi.org/10.1590/s1678-91992011000100002) of body weight.
For garlic, [5 g per kg](https://doi.org/10.2460/ajvr.2000.61.1446) a day for a week damaged red blood cells, though none of the dogs became anaemic.

For example, a poodle of 3.8 kg could be affected by about 60 g of onion, which is less than one small onion.

## What to do?

Call your vet or an animal poison hotline straight away.
Note what was eaten, roughly how much, and when.

### What will the Vet do?

Vets may make your dog vomit if it ate the onion or garlic within the last two hours and has no signs yet, then give activated charcoal.
There is no antidote, so once signs appear the treatment is supportive care.
A severely anaemic dog may need a blood transfusion.
