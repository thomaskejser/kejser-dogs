Antifreeze is toxic to dogs.
Most antifreeze contains **ethylene glycol**, which the body turns into compounds that destroy the kidneys.

Once the kidneys start to fail, most dogs die.

## Signs of antifreeze poisoning

In an experiment, dogs became [dull and wobbly within 1 to 3 hours](https://doi.org/10.2460/ajvr.1984.45.11.2299), and drank and urinated more, but the kidney damage did not show in tests until 48 hours.
Signs seen in [poisoned pets](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.1984.184.01.37) include:

- Wobbling and dullness
- Vomiting
- Low body temperature
- Drinking and urinating more

Don't wait for signs: treatment works only if it starts before the kidneys are damaged.

## How dangerous is it?

Time is what matters most:

- **Dogs treated before their kidneys started to fail** all survived in one study.
- **Dogs whose kidneys had already started to fail** almost all died: [only 1 of 21 survived](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.1996.209.11.1880).
- **Every hour counts**: in an experiment, [all dogs treated at 5 hours](https://doi.org/10.2460/ajvr.1994.55.12.1762) recovered fully, but 2 of 6 dogs treated at 8 hours developed kidney failure.
- **Delays kill**: in an early study of poisoned dogs and cats, 78% died, which the authors blamed on late arrival, diagnosis and treatment.

### At what dose do symptoms appear?

No study has found a safe dose, so treat any amount as an emergency.
In the experiment, 9.5 ml of ethylene glycol per kg of body weight poisoned every dog; for a poodle of 3.8 kg, that is about 36 ml, or two and a half tablespoons.

## What to do?

Go to a vet **immediately** if you think your dog has drunk antifreeze, even a small amount.
Note roughly how much, and when.

### What will the Vet do?

Vets will give an antidote called 4-methylpyrazole (fomepizole) through an IV drip, in several doses over about a day and a half.
It stops the body turning ethylene glycol into its toxic forms, and works best before the kidneys are damaged.
