Grapes and raisins are toxic to dogs.
They can cause sudden **kidney failure**, and the toxic substance is thought to be **tartaric acid**.

Most dogs that eat grapes and are treated quickly never develop kidney damage, but once kidney failure sets in, about [half of dogs die](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1939-1676.2005.tb02744.x).

## Signs of grape and raisin poisoning

- Vomiting: in dogs that developed kidney failure, [81% vomited within 24 hours](https://doi.org/10.1111/jvim.15884) and all within 48 hours
- Lethargy, loss of appetite and diarrhoea
- Producing little or no urine as the kidneys fail
- Wobbling or other neurological signs, seen in most dogs with severe kidney injury

Not every dog vomits first, so don't wait for signs.

## How dangerous is it?

The risk is hard to predict:

- **Most treated dogs stay well**: in [95 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsap.13483) followed after eating grapes, 15% developed signs and one developed kidney injury; in [606 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1111/vec.13025) seen at UK emergency clinics, all survived.
- **Kidney failure is serious**: of [15 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1111/jvim.15884) with severe kidney injury, only eight survived.
- **Some dogs fall seriously ill after a few grapes**, while others eat large amounts without signs.
- **Raisins** have caused poisoning at lower doses, by weight, than fresh grapes, though in a [large study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2026.1812885) fresh grapes were linked to more deaths.
- **Dogs that stop producing urine** have a poor outlook.

### At what dose do symptoms appear?

Kidney damage has been reported after as little as [2.8 g of raisins per kg](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2016.00026) of body weight, and after four to five grapes in an 8.2 kg dog.
Because dogs react so differently, treat any amount as dangerous.

For example, a poodle of 3.8 kg could be poisoned by about 11 g of raisins, which is a small handful.

## What to do?

Call your vet or an animal poison hotline straight away.
Note what was eaten, roughly how much, and when.

### What will the Vet do?

Vets will make your dog vomit and give repeated doses of activated charcoal.
Making a dog vomit is [worthwhile up to 12 hours](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsap.13483) after eating, and removing the fruit early was the [strongest predictor of survival](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2026.1812885).
Your dog will then receive fluids through an IV drip for at least 48 to 72 hours, with its kidney function monitored for at least 72 hours.
