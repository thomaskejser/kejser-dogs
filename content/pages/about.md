# About us - Thomas and Whisky

I like dogs a lot, but I am allergic to them and I never thought I would have the pleasure
of having one as my companion.
This all changed when a friend let me keep Whiskeys older brother "Tequila" with a few days.
To my great pleasure, I learned that my allergy is not triggered by poodles.

In 2025, I got my first dog - a Mini Poodle called: "Whisky".

Pet don't come with a manual - and they can't always tell us how they feel.
We also have a tendency to anthromorphise them: Dog language is not the same as human language.
Ever since Whisky came to live in my house - I have been frantically trying to understand
dogs better - with science and by observing what he does.

Whiskey is intolerant to some proteins and I am currently trying to figure out which ones.
As I am writing this - he is 1.5 years old and has already had three, expensive visits to the hospital.
We love our pets and would do anything for them.
And because we do, I quickly learned that vets - bless them - have grossly misaligned incentives when it comes to providing care for your dog.
Yes, they want to do everything they can to keep Whisky healty.
But, it just so happens that they are also the only vendor in town who sells the hydrolysed,
protein kibble he has to eat during his elimination diet.
And since you are at the hospital anyway - why don't we just sell you this extra medicine "just in case".

But it is not just vets.
Breeders, dog toy/food stores and almost every other dog owner has their own "theory"
of what is best for your dog.
Is it raw food?
Do you need to brush the teeth of your dog?
What should your dog be chewing, are some toys better than others?

The list goes on...

I have a need to know the facts because I am a scientist by heart.
This is why I started this site, to share what I have learned from reading the research
and literature to educate myself to be the best possible guardian for my dear
little Whisky.

I hope you find this useful and I am always curious to learn more.
But let's keep it scientific please - no anecdotes

## How I choose sources

Every answer on this site is backed by research, and every study I cite meets these rules:

- **Peer-reviewed.** I only cite studies published in peer-reviewed scientific journals.
- **No industry funding.** I don't use studies funded by companies that sell products related to the topic, such as pet food, supplements, medicines or grooming products, nor studies written by people employed by such companies.
- **Traceable.** Every study is identified by its DOI or, for older papers without one, its PubMed ID, so you can look it up yourself.
  I check that each link leads to the right paper before adding it.
- **Read, not guessed.** Every claim and number on the site is checked against the study's own abstract or full text.
- **Honest about gaps.** Where no study answers a question, I say so instead of filling the gap with opinion.

Studies marked **Open access** in the reference lists are free for anyone to read.
