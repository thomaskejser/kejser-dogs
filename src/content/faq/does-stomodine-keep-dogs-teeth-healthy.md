# Does Stomodine keep my dog's teeth healthy?

Nobody knows.
I found no published study that tested Stomodine in dogs.
Its main ingredient, the antiseptic **chlorhexidine**, reduces plaque in people, but that tells you little about this product at its dose.

## What is Stomodine?

Stomodine is a range of gels for dogs' and cats' mouths, made by the Italian company ICF, now part of Nextmune.
You squeeze the gel between the lips and gums once or twice a day.

| Product      | What the label lists                                                                                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stomodine    | Chlorhexidine 0.12% and evening primrose oil                                                            |
| Stomodine LP | Chlorhexidine 0.06%, Tris-EDTA (a chemical that helps antiseptics work), safflower oil and glycerin     |
| Stomodine F  | Chlorhexidine "0.07% max", Tris-EDTA, phytosphingosine (a fat found in skin), safflower oil and flavour |

The maker [says Stomodine F](https://www.nextmune.com/products/stomodine-f) "helps to slow the formation of plaque and reduces bad breath".
Some shops also sell it for sore or inflamed gums.

## Has Stomodine been tested?

No, not in any published study that I could find.

- I searched the medical and veterinary databases for Stomodine and for its combination of ingredients, and found no trial.
  I would have included studies paid for by the maker, as long as they were peer-reviewed, but there are none.
- Stomodine is mentioned in two veterinary case reports, as an aftercare gel, but neither tested whether it worked.
- The maker's patent describes a single dog with mouth ulcers that healed after 10 days of treatment.
  There was no comparison, and one dog cannot show what the gel did.
- Stomodine is not on the list of products accepted by the [Veterinary Oral Health Council](https://vohc.org/accepted-products), an independent body that reviews dental products for dogs and cats.

## Does chlorhexidine work?

In people, yes, for plaque.

- A [Cochrane review of 51 trials](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008676.pub2) in people who also brushed their teeth found that chlorhexidine mouthwash gave a **large reduction in plaque**.
- The effect on gum inflammation (**gingivitis**) was small, and the reviewers did not think it was large enough to matter.
- The review found no evidence that one strength of chlorhexidine works better than another, and its results on tartar were inconclusive.
- Chlorhexidine also **stains the teeth** brown: the review found a large increase in staining after 4 weeks or more.

In dogs, the evidence is much thinner.

- Several older studies in laboratory beagles reported that chlorhexidine reduced plaque, but I could not read who funded them, so I don't cite them.
- In a small Indian study, 4 dogs were given [chlorhexidine 0.2% twice a day](https://doi.org/10.14202/vetworld.2019.1872-1876) for 4 weeks after a dental cleaning, and 2 of them still built up new plaque.
  With 4 dogs per group and no statistical comparison, it cannot show whether chlorhexidine helped.

## Why the chlorhexidine evidence may not apply to Stomodine

- **The dose is low.** Stomodine LP and F contain less chlorhexidine than the 0.12–0.2% used in most studies.
- **It goes on the gums, not the teeth.** The studies I found applied chlorhexidine to the teeth or used it as a mouthwash.
- **The other ingredients are untested in dogs' mouths.** I found no study of Tris-EDTA, phytosphingosine or the plant oils for dental health in dogs.

## What the claims rest on

| Claim                         | Evidence                                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Slows plaque                  | Weak and indirect: chlorhexidine cuts plaque in people; untested in dogs |
| Reduces bad breath            | None found                                                               |
| Soothes sore or inflamed gums | None found                                                               |
| Prevents tartar               | Not claimed; results for chlorhexidine in people were inconclusive       |

## What I take from this

Stomodine may slow plaque a little, because chlorhexidine does, but nobody has measured it.
If your dog has bad breath, red gums or tartar, a vet's check and, if needed, a cleaning under anaesthesia come first.
Brushing is the home care vets usually recommend: see [should I brush my dog's teeth?](should-i-brush-my-dogs-teeth.md)
