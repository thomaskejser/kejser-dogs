# Do dogs prefer to pee and poop on some surfaces over others?

Probably, but the evidence is thin.
Dogs given the choice went on real grass rather than artificial turf, droppings in cities pile up on soil rather than paving, and urine collects at the foot of trees and posts.
But only one small study has let dogs choose, and nobody has offered dogs grass and asphalt side by side.

A few older studies that would help are behind paywalls, and I could not read who funded them; they are listed on [papers I am looking for](../pages/papers-i-am-looking-for.md).

## Real grass or artificial turf?

The only choice test I found is small.

- Ten dogs played in a yard with plots of natural grass, reinforced natural grass and artificial turf, ten sessions each.
  They [peed and pooped almost only on natural grass](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16132090); fewer than 10% of pees were on artificial turf.
- The dogs went too rarely for statistics, so the result is a description, not a test.
- The authors suggest the artificial turf was hotter and harder underfoot, but they did not test why the dogs avoided it.

## Grass, earth or paving?

No study has watched pet dogs choose between grass and a hard surface.
What there is comes from counting droppings left behind in cities.

- On the pavements around 26 parks in Buenos Aires, researchers counted 6,080 droppings, and [80% were on the small patch of soil around street trees](https://doi.org/10.1093/jue/juz012), about a square metre each, rather than on the paving.
- That counts what was left, not what dogs chose.
  Owners may steer their dogs to tree pits, or pick up more often from paving, and many of the dogs roamed free.
  The authors say the result can't be generalised to other cities.

## Trees and posts

Urine seems to end up on upright objects by paths, not out on the lawn.

- In 34 parks and green spaces in two Finnish cities, soil [within 30 cm of trees and lamp posts by paths](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2020.615979) held several times more nitrogen than soil a metre away or out on the lawn.
  Lawn beside the path was no different from lawn far from it.
- No dogs were watched; the authors infer from the soil chemistry that dog urine is the source.

## Away from the bed

Where a dog lives matters as well as what it stands on.
In two American shelters, 579 dogs lived in kennels with two compartments, with the bed, food and water on one side.

- On 42% of days the dogs did not pee or poop in the kennel at all.
- When they did, [about three-quarters of pees and poops](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0096254) were on the side away from the bed, food and water.
- Both sides had the same floor, so this is about distance from the den, not the surface.

## Do puppies learn a favourite surface?

Nobody has tested it.
Vets and trainers often say puppies settle on a preferred toilet surface at around eight weeks, but I found no study showing this, or showing that a puppy raised on pads or a kennel floor keeps that preference as an adult.

## Wet, cold or hot ground?

I found no study of dogs refusing to go on wet, cold, snowy or hot ground.
The turf study's suggestion that heat put dogs off artificial turf is the nearest thing.

## What this means

- **Grass or soil is the safe bet.** If you want your dog to go in one place, a patch of real grass or earth is more likely to work than paving or artificial turf, though that rests on very little.
- **Expect trees and posts to be used.** In cities, that is where the urine collects, and dogs often pee over other dogs' marks; see [how do dogs choose where to pee and poop?](how-do-dogs-choose-where-to-pee-and-poop.md)
- **Keep the toilet away from the bed.** Even in a small kennel, most dogs went as far from their bed and food as they could.
