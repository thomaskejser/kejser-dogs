# Which foods are dogs most often allergic to?

Beef and chicken are reported most often, followed by milk, soy, wheat and egg.
But almost every food that has been tested in enough dogs has caused a reaction in some dog, including lamb, fish, rice and duck.
So no food is safe by nature: a "novel" protein is only one your dog has not eaten before.

The most-quoted figures on this topic come from a series of reviews that were funded by a pet-food company, and much of the research on hydrolysed diets was paid for by the companies that sell them.
I don't cite those, so this answer rests on a smaller set of independent studies.

## Allergy or intolerance?

- A **food allergy** is a reaction of the immune system to a food, usually to a protein.
- A **food intolerance** is a reaction that does not involve the immune system, such as trouble digesting a food.

In practice the two cannot be told apart, so vets call both an **adverse food reaction**.
The usual signs are itchy skin, ear and skin infections, and sometimes vomiting or diarrhoea.

## How common is it?

Among 459 dogs seen by a veterinary skin specialist, [about 14% had a food reaction](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-025-04720-0), on its own or together with other skin allergies.
I found no independent figure for dogs in general.

## How is it diagnosed?

With an **elimination diet**: your dog eats only foods it has never had before, or a hydrolysed diet, for several weeks.
If the signs go away, the old foods are given back one at a time (a **food challenge**); a food that brings the signs back is the culprit.

- **Blood tests are not reliable.** In a Brazilian study, results of blood tests for food allergy [did not agree](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0102-09352009000300012) with what happened when the dogs ate the foods.
- **Even food challenges overcall.** In the only blinded, placebo-controlled challenge study I found, 12 allergic dogs were given each food in disguise, alongside dummy meals, without the owners or vets knowing which was which.
  [Half of the dogs also "reacted" to a dummy meal](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.70041), and dummy meals caused reactions about as often as real foods.
  So some foods that owners blame after an open challenge are probably innocent.
- **Getting better on a new diet does not prove an allergy.** In 70 dogs with long-term diarrhoea, some also vomiting, at a Swiss university hospital, 39 improved within 10 days on an elimination diet.
  After 14 weeks, all 39 went back to their old food, and [31 of them stayed well for up to 3 years](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1939-1676.2007.tb03011.x).
  Of the 8 whose signs came back, only 2 clearly reacted when fed beef, lamb, chicken or milk one at a time, with vomiting and bloody stools a day later; the others had only minor signs.
  The dogs that got better on the diet were younger than those that needed steroids (3.5 against 6.5 years on average) and had milder signs, mostly from the large bowel.
- **Signs usually come back within a day.** In 46 Japanese dogs given their old food back after an elimination diet, [61% were itchy again within 12 hours](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.12953), and a quarter within 3 to 6 hours; 7 took 2 to 5 days, and one took 10.
  The authors advise watching the dog for several days after a challenge, not just the first day.
  The itch showed up most often on the legs (57% of dogs) and the face (26%), and no dog had a severe allergic reaction.
  These dogs got their old food back all at once, so the study shows how fast the signs return, not which food caused them.

## Which foods cause reactions?

These five studies confirmed each reaction with an elimination diet and a challenge:

| Study                                                              | Where                          | Dogs | Most common causes                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Jeffers 1996](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.1996.209.03.608)      | USA                            | 25   | Beef 60%, soy 32%, chicken 28%, milk 28%, wheat 24%, egg 20%                                  |
| [Salzo 2009](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0102-09352009000300012)      | Brazil                         | 20   | Beef 55%, chicken 35%, rice 25%                                                               |
| [Hernadi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.70093)                  | Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia | 49   | Chicken 55%, beef 12%, pork 12%, fish 10%, then duck, lamb, soy and milk                      |
| [Gaertner 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci12040383)            | Brazil (only 4 foods tested)   | 11   | Chicken 8 dogs, soy 8, beef 6, pork 5                                                         |
| [Sofou 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.70041) (blinded, 8 foods) | Greece                         | 12   | Chicken 42%; wheat, beef, cod, milk, corn, egg and lamb 33–36% each, close to the dummy meals |

- **Beef and chicken lead** wherever they are common in dog food: chicken in Southeast Asia, beef in the older US and Brazilian studies.
- **Most allergic dogs react to more than one food.** In the US study, dogs reacted to [2.4 foods on average](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.1996.209.03.608), and two thirds reacted to two or more.
- **Grains can cause reactions,** but less often than beef or chicken in the open studies.
- **The ranking does not survive blinding.** When every dog was given every food in disguise, all eight foods came out at roughly the same rate.

Rarer reactions have been reported too:

- **Tomato:** one Japanese dog with cedar-pollen allergy had [swollen lips and drooling after fresh tomato](https://doi.org/10.1292/jvms.64.1069).
- **Gluten:** Irish Setters from affected lines that were raised on wheat [developed gut damage](https://doi.org/10.1136/gut.32.7.749), while their littermates raised gluten-free did not.
  In Border Terriers, a movement disorder has been [linked to gluten](https://doi.org/10.1111/jvim.15038).

## Is there a full list of foods dogs react to?

Not a complete one, but this is what the independent studies show, food by food.
A positive blood test only shows that the immune system recognises a food, not that the dog gets ill from it.

| Evidence                                            | Foods                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Reactions confirmed by a food challenge             | Beef, chicken, milk, egg, soy, wheat, corn, rice, pork, lamb, cod and other fish, duck, tomato                           |
| Positive blood tests only                           | [Potato, pea and pumpkin](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani15213219); horse and hydrolysed diets (see below)                   |
| Only a predicted cross-reaction with a related food | [Barley (with wheat), turkey and rabbit (with chicken), salmon (with chicken), horse](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.13110) |
| No published report found                           | Kangaroo, venison, goat, bison, sweet potato, oats, tapioca, quinoa, coconut, herring, tuna, cricket                     |

"No report found" does not mean safe: it usually means nobody has fed and tested that food in allergic dogs.

### Related foods can cross-react

The proteins that cause reactions are often shared between related foods, so an allergy to one can extend to another.

- **Beef and milk.** Dogs that react to milk often [recognise a beef protein in milk](https://doi.org/10.1292/jvms.21-0162), bovine serum albumin.
- **Meat and fish.** Some allergy-causing proteins in muscle are [similar across chicken, fish, beef and lamb](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.13110), so fish may not be a safe swap for a chicken-allergic dog.
- **Wheat and other plants.** A protein found in grains and potato is predicted to cross-react between wheat, barley, corn, rice, potato and pea.

These are mostly predictions from protein structure; whether they cause symptoms in dogs has not been tested.

### Insects

I found no independent report of a dog reacting to an insect-based food.

- Eight allergic dogs fed a [black soldier fly larvae diet](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci12010068) for 4 weeks did not get worse; that is a small, short study.
- The concern is cross-reaction with house dust mites: antibodies from dogs sensitised to dust mites [recognised mealworm proteins](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani11071942).
  That study found no difference in symptoms, so whether mite-allergic dogs react to insect food is unknown.

### Hydrolysed diets

In a hydrolysed diet, the protein is broken into fragments that are meant to be too small for the immune system to recognise.
Whether these diets are safe for a dog allergic to the original protein has been tested almost only by the companies that sell them.

- **Almost all the trials were paid for by the diet makers.** An independent review found 11 studies of hydrolysed diets in allergic dogs; [10 were sponsored by Purina, Hill's, Royal Canin or Iams](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3164.2009.00761.x), and the eleventh named no sponsor.
  I don't cite those trials directly, but the review is worth reading because its conclusion goes against the sponsors.
- **Less allergenic, not allergen-free.** The review concluded that hydrolysed diets [reduce reactions but don't eliminate them](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3164.2009.00761.x): in the controlled studies, between a fifth and half of the dogs allergic to soy, corn or chicken got worse on a diet made from the hydrolysed version.
  The authors suggest these diets are best used for dogs that are not thought to be allergic to the food they are made from.
  They also warn that a dog that stays itchy on a hydrolysed elimination diet may be wrongly ruled out, when it is really reacting to the diet itself.
- In one independent study, blood from allergic dogs recognised proteins in [hydrolysed fish (6 of 38 dogs), hydrolysed feather (4 of 38) and hydrolysed soy (1 of 28) diets](https://doi.org/10.1111/vde.12536).
  That shows the fragments are not always invisible, but not that the dogs reacted to them.

## Special diets are often contaminated

Diets sold as "limited ingredient", "novel protein" or "hydrolysed" often contain meat that the label does not list.

- In an Italian study, [23 of 40 such diets](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-018-1528-7) contained undeclared animal species, mostly pork, chicken and turkey, including 6 of 9 hydrolysed diets.
- Another Italian study found undeclared species in [6 of 11 limited-ingredient wet foods](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-018-1571-4), but not in the hydrolysed or vegetarian ones.
- In Poland, [4 of 15 ordinary dog foods](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-022-03200-z) contained chicken that the label did not mention.

These studies detected DNA, which does not prove there was enough protein to cause a reaction.
But a contaminated diet can make an elimination diet fail, and make a food look innocent when it is not.

## What I take from this

- **Choose an elimination diet from what your dog has not eaten before**, not from a list of "safe" foods.
- **Avoid close relatives of a confirmed problem food:** beef with milk, chicken with other poultry and possibly fish, wheat with barley.
- **Don't trust blood, hair or saliva tests** to pick the culprit; a food challenge is the only reliable test, and even that can mislead.
- **Don't assume a hydrolysed diet is safe** for a dog allergic to the protein it is made from; some dogs still react.
- **Expect some dogs to react to more than one food.**
- **Ask your vet** before starting an elimination diet, especially for a young dog that needs a complete diet to grow.
