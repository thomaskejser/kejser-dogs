# Will keeping my dog slim help it live longer?

I don't know, and nor does any independent study.
No study free of industry money has measured whether dogs kept slim live longer than dogs that carry extra fat.

What independent research does show is narrower:

- Dogs that are heavy for their breed are more likely to be diagnosed with arthritis.
- In a small trial, obese dogs with arthritis walked better once they had lost weight.
- Many dogs are overweight without anyone recording it, and owners tend to misjudge their own dog's shape.

## Why I can't answer the lifespan question

A [2025 systematic review](https://doi.org/10.4142/jvs.25215) gathered 62 studies on what influences how long pet dogs live.
Three of them looked at body condition or the amount of food and lifespan.
All three were funded by pet food companies or written partly by their staff, so under [how I choose sources](../pages/about.md) I don't use them.

That leaves no study I can use on body weight and lifespan in dogs:

- No independent trial has fed dogs different amounts and followed them until death.
- No independent records study has compared how long lean and overweight pet dogs live.
- No study of any kind has tested whether **slimming down** an already-overweight adult dog lengthens its life.

I won't fill that gap by borrowing results from other species.

## Arthritis

Osteoarthritis is the disease where independent evidence on weight is strongest.

### Heavier dogs are more often diagnosed

- Of [455,557 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-23940-z) seen by UK vets in 2013, about 2.5% had a diagnosis of limb osteoarthritis.
- Dogs at or above the average weight for their breed and sex had 2.3 times the odds of the diagnosis compared with dogs below it.
- The study had only body weight, not body condition, so a heavy dog may be big-boned rather than fat.
  The authors point this out themselves.
- It shows association, not cause.

### Weight loss eased lameness

- In [14 obese dogs with hip or elbow arthritis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11259-010-9348-7) put on a calorie-restricted diet for 16 weeks, lameness eased measurably once they had lost about 6% of their body weight.
  Measurements of how the dogs put weight on their legs confirmed the improvement from about 9% weight loss.
- The trial was small and had no control group, so some of the improvement may have come from other causes.
- It measured lameness, not lifespan.

## How common overweight dogs are

Independent figures depend on who measures and how.

- **What vets write down:** in UK clinical records, only [7.1% of 22,333 dogs](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsap.13325) were recorded as overweight in 2016.
  The authors concluded that vets are under-reporting overweight.
- **In Sweden,** about [30% of dogs](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci10070447) are estimated to be overweight.
- **Risk factors** in the UK records included being neutered, middle-aged and insured, and the breeds at highest risk were [Pugs, Beagles, Golden Retrievers and English Springer Spaniels](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsap.13325).

These are dogs taken to a vet, which may not represent all dogs.

## How to judge your dog's body condition

Weight alone does not tell you whether a dog is fat, because dogs of one breed vary in size.
Vets use a **body condition score** instead.
On the usual nine-point scale, 1 to 3 is underweight, [4 to 5 is normal weight](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci10070447), 6 to 7 overweight and 8 to 9 obese.

In a [Swedish study](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci10070447), owners were taught to look and feel at three places:

- **Ribs:** how much fat you feel over them.
- **Waist:** whether the dog narrows behind the ribs, seen from above.
- **Belly line:** whether the belly tucks up towards the hind legs, seen from the side.

What the same study found:

- **Owners tend to score too low.**
  Owners underscored their own dog in 35% of 82 assessments, most often when the dog was overweight.
  On average, owners scored their dogs at 4.6 and vet staff at 5.2.
- **A short lesson fixes most of it.**
  After a 5 to 10 minute lesson on the scale, owners scored as well as the vet staff.
- **Owners misjudge normal dogs too.**
  Shown a photo of a normal-weight dog, 564 owners scored it at 3.9 on average, as if it were slightly thin.

The owners were not chosen at random, so the results may not hold for all owners.

## What is known and what is not

**Known, as association:** dogs heavy for their breed are more often diagnosed with arthritis, in a large independent records study.

**Shown in a small uncontrolled trial:** obese dogs with arthritis walked better after losing about 6 to 9% of their weight.

**Not known from independent research:**

- whether a slim dog lives longer than an overweight one;
- whether slimming an overweight dog lengthens its life;
- how much extra fat, if any, is harmless.

## What to do

These steps follow from the studies above; none of them has been shown to lengthen a dog's life.

- Ask your vet to show you how to score your dog's body condition, since a short lesson made owners as accurate as vet staff.
- If your dog is overweight and has arthritis, ask your vet for a weight-loss plan; a loss of around 6 to 9% eased lameness in the small trial.
- Watch breeds prone to weight gain, such as Pugs, Beagles and Golden Retrievers, and dogs after neutering.
