# Is caffeine toxic to dogs?

Caffeine is toxic to dogs.
It belongs to the same family of stimulants as theobromine in [chocolate](is-chocolate-toxic.md).

High doses can cause seizures and death from heart rhythm problems or breathing failure.

## Signs of caffeine poisoning

Signs can appear within a couple of hours.
In the dogs that ate the caffeine supplement, 80% showed signs within 8 hours, and signs lasted up to 48 hours:

- Restlessness, hyperactivity and behaviour changes
- Tremors and seizures
- Vomiting
- A racing heartbeat
- Raised body temperature

## How dangerous is it?

The risk depends on how much caffeine is eaten and the size of the dog:

- **Sources** include coffee, coffee beans and grounds, tea, energy drinks and caffeine tablets.
- **Small dogs** are poisoned by smaller amounts, since the danger depends on the dose per kg of body weight.
- **Serious cases can be fatal**: of [47 dogs](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.2001.218.225) that ate a supplement containing caffeine and the stimulant ephedrine, 17% died or were put to sleep.

### At what dose do symptoms appear?

Poison centre data show mild symptoms at [20 mg/kg](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2016.00026) of caffeine and theobromine combined, severe symptoms at 40 to 50 mg/kg, and seizures at 60 mg/kg.
The dose that kills half of dogs is reported as about [140 mg/kg](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13181-012-0254-y) of caffeine.

For example, a poodle of 3.8 kg would show symptoms after about 76 mg of caffeine, which is less than a typical cup of coffee.
About 530 mg could be fatal to such a dog.

## What to do?

Call your vet or an animal poison hotline straight away.
Note what was eaten or drunk, roughly how much, and when.

### What will the Vet do?

Vets will try to empty your dog's stomach, by making it vomit or by washing it out, and give repeated doses of activated charcoal.
They will then provide careful supportive care, with which most dogs recover.
The outlook is usually good if this happens within two to four hours of eating.

In a massive overdose, caffeine can be filtered out of the blood: in one dog, [dialysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/vms3.1147) removed 89% of it.
