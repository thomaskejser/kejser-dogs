# What counts as a teacup, toy, miniature or standard (regular) poodle?

The American Kennel Club and the FCI treat all poodles as one breed; the UK has a separate standard for each size.
Kennel clubs split them into size varieties by **height at the withers**, the highest point of the shoulders, measured with the dog standing.
None of the breed standards sets a weight.

## The official sizes

| Variety        | American Kennel Club (US)        | Royal Kennel Club (UK) | FCI (most of Europe)                  |
| -------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Toy            | 25.4 cm (10 in) or less          | Under 28 cm (11 in)    | Over 24 cm up to 28 cm; ideal 25      |
| Miniature      | Over 25.4 cm up to 38 cm (15 in) | 28 cm to under 38 cm   | Over 28 cm up to 35 cm                |
| Medium (Moyen) | Not a variety                    | Not a variety          | Over 35 cm up to 45 cm                |
| Standard       | Over 38 cm (15 in)               | Over 38 cm (15 in)     | Over 45 cm up to 60 cm, +2 cm allowed |

The sources are the [AKC standard](https://images.akc.org/pdf/breeds/standards/Poodle.pdf), the UK standards for the [Toy](https://www.royalkennelclub.com/breed-standards/utility/poodle-toy/), [Miniature](https://www.royalkennelclub.com/breed-standards/utility/poodle-miniature/) and [Standard](https://www.royalkennelclub.com/breed-standards/utility/poodle-standard/) poodle, and [FCI standard No. 172](https://www.fci.be/Nomenclature/Standards/172g09-en.pdf).

## Where the clubs differ

- **The toy limit.**
  A 27 cm poodle is a toy in the UK and under the FCI, but a miniature in the US.
- **The medium.**
  Only the FCI has a fourth size.
  A 40 cm poodle is a standard in the US and UK, but a medium under the FCI.
- **Limits at the extremes.**
  The AKC and the Royal Kennel Club set no maximum for standards and no minimum for toys.
  The FCI disqualifies standards taller than 62 cm and toys shorter than 23 cm.

"Regular" poodle is not an official term; it usually means a standard.

## Does it matter how big the parents are?

In the breed standards, a poodle's size is its own adult height, whatever its parents' size.
The parents are a guide, not a guarantee.

- **Size depends on many genes.**
  Variants at six genes explain [about half the size difference](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.157339.113) between breeds, and most breeds carry a mix of them rather than one fixed set.
  So two toy parents cannot guarantee a puppy that stays within the toy limit.
- **American Kennel Club.**
  The [registration rules](https://images.akc.org/pdf/rulebooks/RREGS4.pdf) list "Poodles" once, with no varieties, so the registration does not fix a size.
  The variety is named when a dog is entered at a show, and under the [show rules](https://images.akc.org/pdf/rulebooks/RREGS3.pdf) the judge can measure it with a wicket.
  A dog outside its variety's limits is disqualified, and after three such disqualifications it may not be shown again.
  A poodle from toy parents that grows over 10 inches can therefore not compete as a toy, but nothing in these rules stops it being shown as a miniature.
- **FCI.**
  Poodle sizes are on the FCI's [list of varieties that may be crossed](https://www.fci.be/nomenclature/docs/SCI-REG-CRO-RAC-VAR.pdf), with permission from the national kennel club, and only with the nearest size because of the risk of whelping problems.
  How a puppy's size is recorded on its pedigree is left to each national club; the FCI's breeding rules and crossing list set no common rule for it.
- **Royal Kennel Club (UK).**
  Under its [registration regulations](https://www.royalkennelclub.com/about-us/about-the-rkc/what-we-do/the-royal-kennel-club-rules-regulations-and-codes/registration-rules-and-regulations/), a dog normally enters the Breed Register only if its parents are registered as the same breed, with exceptions for breeds listed in an annex that is not published on that page.
  The published rules therefore do not show whether litters from two different poodle sizes can be registered in the UK, or whether a dog can move to another size's register when it grows out of its own.

## What is a teacup poodle?

"Teacup" is not a variety in any of these standards.
It is a marketing term for toy poodles bred to be unusually small, often well under the toy limit.
Under the AKC and UK standards such a dog is simply a toy; under the FCI, one shorter than 23 cm is disqualified.
The FCI also disqualifies any poodle with signs of dwarfism, such as a domed skull or bulging eyes, and toys with very light bone.

## What the research shows

There are few studies of "teacup" dogs as such, so the evidence on their health is indirect.

- **Small size has a common genetic basis.**
  Size-reducing versions of six growth genes, including [IGF1](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.157339.113), are rare in wolves, and the more of them a dog carries, the smaller it tends to be.
- **Being smaller than the breed average carries risk.**
  In over 200,000 dogs seen by vets in England, dogs lighter than the average for their breed and sex had [1.4 times the odds](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40575-016-0034-0) of patellar luxation, a kneecap that slips out of place and can cause lameness and arthritis.
  Breeds with the highest odds included the Pomeranian, Chihuahua and Yorkshire Terrier.

If you buy a toy poodle, ask the breeder for the parents' heights, and be wary of anyone who sells dogs by weight or by the word "teacup".
