# About us - Thomas and Whisky

I like dogs a lot, but I am allergic to them, and I never thought I would have the pleasure of having one as my companion.
This all changed when a friend let me keep a mini poodle called "Tequila" in my home for a few days.
I did not have an allergic reaction!

In 2025, I got my first dog, from the same mother as "Tequila".
Meet "Whisky", my poodle:

![My dog Whisky](/img/pages/about/whisky.webp)

This is when I realised I had NO idea how to take care of this little guy.
Pets don't come with a user manual.
We also tend to anthropomorphise them: dog language is not the same as human language, and our furry friends can't always tell us what is wrong.
Ever since Whisky moved into my house, I have been frantically trying to understand him better, with science and by observing what he does.
The site you are on is the result of this search for truth.

## Misaligned Incentives

Whisky is intolerant of at least one protein.
I am currently trying to figure out which ones.
As I write this, he is 1.5 years old and has already had three expensive visits to the hospital.
We love our pets and would do anything for them, and vets know this.
Unfortunately, pet hospitals are big businesses that aren't funded by the taxpayer (at least not here in Estonia).

I trust that the vets I have used want to do everything they can to keep Whisky healthy.
But it just so happens that they are also the only vendor in town that sells the hydrolysed protein kibble he has to eat during his elimination diet.
It comes from the same pet-food companies that sponsor much of the research.
And since you are at the hospital anyway, why not sell you some extra medicine "just in case"?
I found the advice highly generic and often focused on treating symptoms rather than finding the root cause of the problem.

Breeders, dog toy and food stores, and almost every other dog owner also have their own "theory" of what is best for your dog.
Is it raw food or kibble?
And then there is the hippie nonsense about "natural" foods (hint: your dog is already the most unnatural, genetically engineered creature to walk this planet).
Do you need to brush your dog's teeth, or is chewing enough?
WHAT should your dog be chewing? Are some toys better than others?

The list goes on...

I need to know the facts, because I am a scientist at heart and because I am the guardian of a little guy who needs me.
This is why I started this site: to share what I have learned from reading the research and literature and educating myself.

I hope you find this useful.
I am always curious to learn more, and I would like your feedback and comments.
But let's keep it scientific, please: no anecdotes.

## How I choose sources

Every answer on this site is backed by research, and every study I cite meets these rules:

- **Peer-reviewed.** I only cite studies published in peer-reviewed scientific journals.
- **No industry funding.** I don't use studies funded by companies that sell products related to the topic, such as pet food, supplements, medicines or grooming products, nor studies written by people employed by such companies.
- **Traceable.** Every study is identified by its DOI or, for older papers without one, its PubMed ID, so you can look it up yourself.
  - I check that each link leads to the right paper before adding it.
  - Every claim and number on the site is checked against the study's own abstract or full text.
- **Honest about gaps.** Where no study answers a question, I say so instead of filling the gap with opinion.

Studies marked **Open access** in the reference lists are free for anyone to read.
